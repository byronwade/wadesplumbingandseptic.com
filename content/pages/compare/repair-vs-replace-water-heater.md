A cold shower or a damp garage floor forces a fast decision: fix the water heater you have, or replace it. Age, leak location, and code requirements matter more than a one-size slogan.

## Repair often makes sense when

- The unit is relatively young and the failure is a part (thermostat, element, thermocouple, anode, valve)
- There is no tank leak and the heater still holds pressure
- Venting, gas, and electrical are already correct for the existing unit
- You need hot water restored the same day while you plan a later upgrade

## Replacement is usually smarter when

- The tank is leaking, or rust is staining the base and pan
- The heater is at or past typical service life and efficiency is poor
- You are remodeling or adding bathrooms and the old unit is undersized
- A tankless retrofit is justified by gas/electrical capacity and real usage patterns
- Current code requires straps, expansion tanks, or venting updates that pair naturally with a new install

Coastal garages and damp mountain crawlspaces shorten tank life. Well water with sediment can do the same if the tank is never flushed.

## Tank vs tankless is a separate choice

Replacing a failed tank with another tank is often the cleanest path. Tankless can be excellent when the home has the right fuel supply and venting, and when simultaneous hot-water demand is the real pain point. It is a poor upsell when the gas line or electrical service cannot support it.
