## Monthly checks

- Look under sinks and around toilets for moisture.
- Listen for toilets that continue running after a flush.
- Check exposed supply hoses for corrosion, bulges, or cracking.
- Make sure slow drains are addressed before they become complete blockages.

## Every six months

- Test the main water shutoff and accessible fixture stops.
- Clean faucet aerators and showerheads.
- Inspect the water heater area for rust, moisture, or unusual sounds.
- Confirm exterior drains remain clear before the rainy season.

## Annual professional maintenance

- Test water pressure and confirm it remains below 80 PSI.
- Flush and inspect the water heater as the manufacturer recommends.
- Service tankless water heaters for scale and combustion condition.
- Inspect aging supply hoses and accessible piping.
- Review septic alarms, pumps, controls, and risers.

## Septic pumping and inspections

Many residential septic tanks need pumping every 3 to 5 years, but the correct interval depends on tank size, occupancy, usage, and system type. Advanced systems may require more frequent inspection or manufacturer-specific service.

## Call before a small issue grows

Recurring clogs, water-pressure changes, sewage odors, damp soil, unexplained water usage, or a water heater that behaves differently are all reasons to schedule an evaluation.
