A shower valve controls more than on and off. Depending on the design, it may balance pressure, limit maximum temperature, control volume, or maintain a thermostatic set point.

## Common symptoms

- Temperature changes when another fixture runs
- Weak flow at every shower-head setting
- A handle that is hard to turn
- Dripping after shutoff
- Inability to reach or limit hot temperature
- Corroded trim or inaccessible replacement parts

## Diagnose before replacing the valve

Low flow may come from the shower head, cartridge, integral stops, supply pressure, undersized piping, or debris. Temperature problems may involve the cartridge, limit stop, crossed piping, water heater, or recirculation system.

## Replacement planning

A concealed valve must be mounted at the correct depth and supported securely. The new rough valve, trim, cartridge, tub spout, and shower components must be compatible. Wall access and finish repair should be discussed before work starts.

After installation, test hot and cold operation, temperature limit, flow, shutoff, and every visible connection.
