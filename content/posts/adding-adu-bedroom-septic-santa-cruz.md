Adding a bedroom or an accessory dwelling unit (ADU) on a Santa Cruz County septic system is an Environmental Health review, not only a building permit. County upgrade standards apply when the house will have more bedrooms, more than 500 extra square feet, another structure, or an ADU beyond what the existing septic permit covers. ## Quick answer

- Look up the parcel in Environmental Health's public files. The septic permit lists the structures, bedrooms, and square footage the system was approved for.
- A one-time addition of up to 500 square feet with no extra bedrooms can stay on repair standards if the current house still matches that permit.
- An ADU, extra bedrooms, or more than 500 added square feet brings in upgrade standards. The system has to meet (or be upgraded to meet) current rules for the total bedrooms and dwelling units.
- An ADU is a separate unit for design flow. A detached ADU may have its own tank or share a combined system that is sized for both buildings.
- Unincorporated county rules are in Chapter 7.38. If you live in [Santa Cruz](/service-area/santa-cruz-ca-plumbing-septic-services), [Capitola](/service-area/capitola-ca-plumbing-septic-services), [Scotts Valley](/service-area/scotts-valley-ca-plumbing-septic-services), or [Watsonville](/service-area/watsonville-ca-plumbing-septic-services), confirm the septic path with that city's building department as well.

## Start with the permit, not the floor plan

Santa Cruz County Environmental Health tells owners to check whether the parcel has a permitted onsite wastewater treatment system (OWTS) before deciding whether repair or upgrade standards apply. The county's [EH Files](https://scceh.com/NewHome/EnvironmentalHealthDocuments.aspx) show the most recent permit: number of structures, bedrooms, and square footage. If a legal ADU already exists, there will be two permits to consider. A parcel with no permitted system has to meet current standards for new development.

That record is the baseline. How you use a room today does not replace it. For the step-by-step install path after the design is approved, see [new septic installations in Santa Cruz County](/new-septic-installations-santa-cruz).

## Repair versus upgrade

County Environmental Health defines a **repair** as replacing an old or malfunctioning permitted system that is still the right size for the house. **Upgrade** is a modification of a permitted system, including adding dispersal area or treatment components for a remodel, or installing an additional system or tank for an ADU on a developed parcel.

On the county's [repair and upgrade page](https://scceh.com/NewHome/Programs/LandUse/OWTSRepairUpgrades.aspx), repair standards apply if the current structures do not exceed what the OWTS was permitted for, with a one-time square-footage allowance per parcel if no bedrooms are added. Upgrade standards apply if the current structure has more bedrooms, more habitable area than that allowance, or more structures than the OWTS permit allowed.

County Code Chapter 7.38.080 sets that allowance. A one-time addition of up to 500 square feet of habitable space with no increase in bedrooms may be approved with no change to the existing system if the addition does not encroach on the system or expansion area, the county has adequate information on the system, the one-time limit (which started January 1, 1993) has not already been used, and the system is functioning without failure.

Additions of more than 500 square feet, extra bedrooms, or an ADU under Chapter 13.10 may be approved only if the OWTS meets, or is upgraded to meet, current standard or enhanced-treatment requirements for the **total** number of bedrooms and dwelling units in the project, including the existing ones.

Simple interior work that does not add bedrooms or change the building footprint (rewiring, replumbing, reroofing, or a foundation replacement with no footprint change) does not need Environmental Health review. Anything that increases site disturbance or potential wastewater flow does.

## What counts as a bedroom

Do not guess from furniture. County Environmental Health's repair and upgrade page describes a bedroom, for this review, as any room other than a kitchen, bathroom, and one living space. County Code Chapter 7.38 also defines bedroom for OWTS sizing as any room that could be used as a bedroom as determined by the Health Officer, including any room at least 70 square feet that can furnish the isolation needed for sleeping.

The Health Officer's determination is what the permit uses. If a den, office, or loft meets that test, it can count even if no one sleeps there now. Pull the existing permit and ask the designer to confirm the bedroom count the county will use.

## How an ADU is sized

Chapter 7.38 treats an ADU as a **separate unit** for design flow. Installing an additional or expanded OWTS to serve an ADU on a developed parcel is an upgrade. For a new detached ADU, the second unit may have its own OWTS if the main house system meets standards, or the two buildings may share a combined system sized for the combined flows, with each unit served by its own septic tank. A combined tank may be allowed on an enhanced treatment system if it meets the design-flow specification.

The county sizes dispersal area from expected flow based on bedrooms (or rooms that could be used as bedrooms). Standard design flow in Chapter 7.38 is:

| Bedrooms in that unit | Standard design flow (gallons per day) |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| 1                     | 250                                    |
| 2                     | 300                                    |
| 3                     | 375                                    |
| 4                     | 450                                    |
| 5                     | 525                                    |
| 6                     | 600                                    |
| Each bedroom after 6  | add 75                                 |

Because an ADU is its own unit, a one-bedroom ADU is not simply "one more bedroom" on the main house table. The designer calculates flow for each dwelling, then sizes tanks and dispersal for that total. Enhanced treatment that lowers BOD and TSS below 30 mg/L can allow a higher application rate, which is why many Santa Cruz County ADU upgrades use an engineered unit. See [engineered septic systems](/what-you-need-to-know-about-engineered-septic-systems) and [new septic options in Santa Cruz County](/a-homeowners-guide-to-new-septic-systems-in-santa-cruz-county).

An existing dispersal system approved before June 1, 2018 may be credited toward a bedroom addition if it meets groundwater separation, well setback, stream setback, and dispersal-area rules. Trenches with a flow depth between 2 and 10 feet may be credited at up to 10 square feet per linear foot only if a qualified professional shows the field can take the extra flow without harming water quality. Deeper systems, or systems that miss other requirements, may be used only with enhanced treatment. That is a site-specific finding, not a promise that an old leach field will pass.

## Who designs, who installs

County Environmental Health requires a qualified professional for site evaluation, soil work, percolation tests, and the system design: a California registered civil engineer, professional geologist, registered environmental health specialist, or another professional the county approves. Slope-stability reports come from a licensed professional geologist or certified engineering geologist. The installer must hold a California contractor license in Class A, Class B, C-36, or C-42. A Class B holder may install a system only as part of a new construction project.

A sewage disposal permit is required to upgrade, enlarge, replace, repair, abandon, or destroy a system, whether the applicant is an owner, contractor, or public agency. The application is accepted only when it is complete: the design with a scaled plot plan and test results, fees, and any grading plan, easement letter, slope report, or geotechnical report the county asks for. If the dispersal area or expansion area is on slopes over 30 percent, a technical report is required.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I add an ADU if my house is on septic in Santa Cruz County?

Yes, if the OWTS meets or is upgraded to meet current standards for the total bedrooms and dwelling units, including the ADU. County Environmental Health reviews that before the building permit is issued. The existing permit in EH Files is the starting point.

### Does converting a garage or office into a bedroom trigger a septic upgrade?

It can. Extra bedrooms beyond what the septic permit allowed bring in upgrade standards. The Health Officer decides what counts as a bedroom for sizing. Have the designer compare the proposed rooms with the permitted count.

### Does Wade's pump the tank before an ADU upgrade?

No. Wade's installs septic systems alongside the project's engineer and does not pump tanks. If the tank needs to be emptied, a county-permitted liquid waste hauler does that work.
