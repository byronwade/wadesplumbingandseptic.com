# A Simple Home Plumbing Checklist Before Hosting Guests

Hosting family or friends can mean more activity in kitchens, bathrooms, and laundry areas. A little preparation can help you spot small plumbing concerns before they become inconvenient during a gathering.

## Check Guest Bathrooms

Start with the bathroom guests are most likely to use.

- Flush the toilet once or twice and make sure it refills normally.
- Run the sink faucet and look beneath the cabinet for visible moisture or drips.
- Confirm that hot and cold water are available at the faucet.
- Test the bathroom fan, if present, to help manage moisture.
- Place a small waste bin near the toilet and make sure toilet paper is easy to find.
- Clear hair, soap, and other debris from the sink drain stopper.

Avoid pouring chemical drain cleaners into a slow drain before guests arrive. These products can be harsh, may not solve the underlying issue, and can create complications if further work is needed.

## Give Kitchen Drains Some Attention

The kitchen often sees extra use when meals and drinks are being prepared. Check the sink before cooking begins.

- Run water in the sink and watch for slow drainage.
- Look under the sink for signs of leaking around supply lines, drain connections, or the disposal.
- Empty the sink strainer and remove food scraps that could restrict water flow.
- Make sure dishes, serving trays, and other items are not stacked in a way that blocks the drain.
- Run the dishwasher ahead of time if it will be used, and check the surrounding floor for moisture afterward.

Keep grease, cooking oil, coffee grounds, and large food scraps out of the drain. Let cooking grease cool in a suitable container and dispose of it according to local household waste guidance.

## Look for Small Leaks and Moisture

A quick walk-through can help identify areas that deserve attention before visitors arrive.

Check around:

- Toilet bases and shutoff valves
- Sink cabinets
- Shower and tub edges
- Water-using appliances
- Exposed pipes in utility spaces

Pay attention to damp cabinet floors, water stains, musty odors, or unexplained puddles. If you find active leaking, avoid trying to force fittings tighter or disassemble plumbing parts without the right knowledge. Turn off the nearby fixture’s water supply only if you can do so safely and confidently, then arrange for appropriate help.

## Help Prevent Drain Clogs During the Visit

Simple reminders and a few supplies can make plumbing easier for everyone using the home.

Consider placing these items where they are useful:

- A small bin in each bathroom
- A sink strainer in the kitchen
- Paper towels or cleaning cloths for small spills
- Clear access to toilet paper and hand soap

For toilets, only toilet paper and normal waste should be flushed. Items such as wipes, tissues, cotton products, dental floss, and hygiene products can contribute to clogs even when packaging suggests they are flushable.

In the kitchen, scrape plates into the trash or compost before rinsing them. This reduces the amount of food material entering the drain.

## Know Where Important Shutoffs Are

It is helpful to know the location of your home’s main water shutoff valve before a busy day. You may also want to identify individual shutoff valves beneath sinks and behind toilets.

Do not wait until there is a problem to locate them. A quick review can make it easier to respond calmly if a fixture begins leaking. Keep the area around the main shutoff accessible and avoid storing heavy items directly in front of it.
