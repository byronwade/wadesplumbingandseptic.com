On City of Santa Cruz Water, Soquel Creek Water District, and San Lorenzo Valley Water District accounts, the property owner owns the pipe from the meter to the house. The utility owns the main, the line to the meter, and the meter itself. A leak, wet yard, or unexplained extra use on the customer side is plumbing work. ## Quick answer

- **Utility side:** water main, service pipe to the meter, meter, and meter box. Call the water provider if the leak is in the box or between the box and the main.
- **Owner side:** everything after the meter, including the connection at the meter and the run to the house. That is true in [Santa Cruz](/service-area/santa-cruz-ca-plumbing-septic-services), in [Capitola](/service-area/capitola-ca-plumbing-septic-services) (Soquel Creek), and in [Felton](/service-area/felton-ca-plumbing-septic-services) (SLVWD).
- Shut water off at the **property-side** valve. City of Santa Cruz rules say customers must not operate the city-owned control valve.
- Replacing or rearranging that piping needs a plumbing permit in unincorporated Santa Cruz County. Cities issue their own permits.

## Where the utility's pipe ends

**City of Santa Cruz.** Municipal Code section 16.04.070 says the city owns the facilities between the meter and the water main, including the meter and meter box, even when they sit on private property. The city maintains those parts. Everything on the property side of the meter, including the connection from the meter, belongs to the property owner to repair, replace, and maintain. Section 16.04.060 requires a control valve on the property side and says customers shall not operate the city-owned valve.

**Soquel Creek Water District** ([Capitola](/service-area/capitola-ca-plumbing-septic-services), [Soquel](/service-area/soquel-ca-plumbing-septic-services), [Aptos](/service-area/aptos-ca-plumbing-septic-services), and other district accounts). Ordinance 25-01 states that the customer's responsibility begins at the water meter. The district maintains the service connection from the main to the meter, the meter spud, and the meter box. Repair and replacement of equipment on the customer side of the meter (not including the meter spud) is the customer's responsibility. Water lost through failing customer equipment is billed as consumption.

**San Lorenzo Valley Water District** ([Felton](/service-area/felton-ca-plumbing-septic-services), [Ben Lomond](/service-area/ben-lomond-ca-plumbing-septic-services), [Boulder Creek](/service-area/boulder-creek-ca-plumbing-septic-services), and other SLVWD accounts). SLVWD's meters-and-service FAQ says the meter is the end of district ownership. Problems at the meter or between the meter and the main go to the district office. A leak between the meter and the home is the homeowner's to repair. District rules (section 3.09) keep facilities from the main to the meter outlet as district property, and the piping beyond the meter outlet as the customer's.

Other providers (including city of [Watsonville](/service-area/watsonville-ca-plumbing-septic-services) water and [Scotts Valley](/service-area/scotts-valley-ca-plumbing-septic-services) Water District) publish their own rules. Confirm ownership with the name on your water bill before anyone digs.

## Signs the leak is on your side

These point to the customer-side run, not a fixture drip:

- A wet or unusually green strip between the meter and the house
- The meter still turning after every fixture is off (see [hidden leak signs](/6-signs-you-have-a-hidden-water-leak))
- Low pressure at several fixtures, not just one
- Water sound along the service path when nothing is running

If water is in the meter box, or you cannot tell which side of the meter is wet, call the utility first. SLVWD and the city both treat the meter and its fittings as theirs.

Do not operate the utility's shutoff at the curb. Use the house or property-side valve. If that valve fails, the utility has to operate its own stop.

## Permits and what the plumber does

County Building Permits says a plumbing permit is required when plumbing piping is installed, altered, repaired, or replaced, unless the California Plumbing Code or a county ordinance exempts the work. Stopping a leak without replacing or rearranging pipe is listed as exempt. Replacing the service line is not. Cities such as Santa Cruz, Capitola, Scotts Valley, and Watsonville issue their own plumbing permits.

A typical customer-side job:

1. Confirm which utility serves the parcel and that the leak is past the meter.
2. Camera or locate the line, then explain a spot repair versus a full replacement.
3. Pull the plumbing permit for replacement work.
4. Coordinate a utility meter shutoff if the property-side valve cannot isolate the line.
5. Repair or replace, inspect, and restore.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who pays to replace the water line from the meter to my house?

The property owner, on City of Santa Cruz, Soquel Creek, and SLVWD accounts. The utility pays for the main, the utility-owned service to the meter, and the meter, unless the owner damaged those parts.

### Can I shut the water off at the meter myself?

Use the valve on the house side of the meter. City of Santa Cruz code says customers shall not operate the city-owned control valve. If you have no working property-side valve, call the utility to operate theirs.

### Do I need a permit to replace a leaking water service line?

In unincorporated Santa Cruz County, yes, if the repair replaces or rearranges piping. Stopping a leak without replacement is listed as exempt. Confirm the rule with your city building department if you live inside city limits.
