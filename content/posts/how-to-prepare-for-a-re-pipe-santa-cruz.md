A re-pipe replaces the water supply lines inside the house, not one leaky fitting. You prepare by confirming the pipe material, making access to walls, crawl spaces or the attic, lining up a plumbing permit, and planning for the water to be off while the new lines are installed. ## Quick answer

- Repair one section when the rest of the piping is sound.
- Re-pipe when low pressure, rusty water or leaks show up throughout the house, especially on old galvanized lines.
- Unincorporated Santa Cruz County requires a plumbing permit to replace pipes. Cities have their own building departments.
- Know the main shutoff, clear access, and decide which fixtures stay before the crew arrives.

## Signs the supply lines, not one fixture, are the problem

- Low water pressure at several faucets after aerators are clean
- Rusty or discolored water that is not coming from the water heater alone
- Pinholes or leaks that keep appearing on the same runs
- Visible galvanized (gray, threaded) pipe in a crawl space, garage or utility room
- A water meter that moves when every fixture is off, pointing to a hidden supply leak

One dripping faucet is usually a washer or cartridge. Whole-house symptoms are a piping problem. Our [hidden leak guide](/6-signs-you-have-a-hidden-water-leak) covers the meter test. [Pipe repair and replacement](/service-offerings/pipe-repair-and-replacement) is the service page for both a single section and a re-pipe.

## Galvanized pipe and lead, without guessing about your house

EPA lists galvanized pipe as a source of lead in drinking water: lead particles can attach to the inside of galvanized pipe and later come off into the water. EPA also publishes [Protect Your Tap: A Quick Check for Lead](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/protect-your-tap-quick-check-lead-0), a homeowner walkthrough for finding lead pipes.

That does not mean every Santa Cruz County home has a lead service line. Ask your water utility whether it has a service line inventory for your address. Indoor galvanized that is leaking, rusting or choking flow is a separate reason to re-pipe the house side, which you own.

## Permit: replacement is not the same as stopping a leak

Santa Cruz County Building Permits says a plumbing permit is required when plumbing, gas or drainage piping, a fixture, or water heating or treatment equipment is installed, altered, repaired, or replaced, unless the California Plumbing Code or County ordinance exempts the work. Exempt work includes stopping leaks in drains, soil, waste or vent pipe, clearing stoppages, and repairing leaks in pipes, valves or fixtures when the repair does not replace or rearrange those parts.

A whole-house re-pipe replaces and rearranges piping, so it needs a permit in the unincorporated county. If you live in Santa Cruz, Capitola, Scotts Valley or Watsonville, confirm the rule with that city's building department. The installer should pull the permit for the work they do.

## What to decide before the quote

- **Access.** Can someone reach the crawl space, attic, garage walls and fixture boxes? Boxes stored in those spaces slow the job.
- **Fixtures that stay.** A re-pipe often stops at the shutoff under a sink or behind a toilet. If you also want new faucets, toilets or a water heater, say so up front so the quote covers it. See [fixture installation](/service-offerings/fixture-installation) and [repair or replace a water heater](/should-you-repair-or-replace-your-water-heater).
- **Pipe material.** Copper and PEX are both used for residential supply lines. The right choice depends on the house, local code, water quality and how the lines are routed. Ask the plumber to explain the option for your layout rather than picking a material from an ad.
- **Water-off window.** A re-pipe is not a one-hour repair. Ask for the planned shutoff window when you get the quote, and plan cooking, showers and pets around it.
- **Main shutoff.** Everyone in the house should know where it is before the work starts.

## How to get the house ready

1. Photograph the water heater, under-sink shutoffs, the crawl space and any exposed pipe. Those photos help the quote and later service.
2. Move storage away from access hatches, the water heater and under-sink cabinets.
3. Leave a parking space for the crew and keep pets in a closed room.
4. If you have irrigation, a well, or a backflow preventer, point it out. Those systems are easy to miss on a first walkthrough. Annual testing of a backflow assembly is a separate requirement; see [backflow testing](/backflow-testing-santa-cruz-county-homes).
5. Plan to be reachable during office hours, Monday to Friday, 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM, in case a fixture choice or shutoff location needs a decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a re-pipe the same as replacing a sewer line?

No. A re-pipe replaces the drinking-water supply piping. A sewer or drain line carries wastewater out of the house. Those are different permits, materials and access. Recurring drain backups belong on a [sewer camera inspection](/sewer-camera-inspection-santa-cruz-homes), not a re-pipe.

### Do I need a permit to re-pipe in Santa Cruz County?

In the unincorporated county, replacing pipes requires a plumbing permit. Repairing a leak without replacing or rearranging the pipe can be exempt. Confirm the rule with your city if you are inside city limits.

### Can I re-pipe one bathroom and leave the rest?

Sometimes. If only one branch is failing and the rest of the piping is sound, a targeted replacement can be enough. If galvanized is throughout the house, replacing one bathroom leaves the rest of the problem in the walls.
