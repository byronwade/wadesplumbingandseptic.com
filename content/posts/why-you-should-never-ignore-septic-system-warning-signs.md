Septic problems rarely improve on their own. Slow drains, alarms, odors, wet soil, and backups are the system's way of showing that flow, treatment, or dispersal is not working correctly.

## Warning signs that deserve attention

- Several fixtures draining slowly
- Gurgling after a toilet flush
- A pump or high-water alarm
- Sewage odor in the house or yard
- Wet, soft, or unusually green soil over the system
- Wastewater backing up at the lowest fixture

## Why waiting costs more

Continued use can push solids into the field, damage pumps, flood electrical components, contaminate soil, and turn a serviceable problem into a replacement project.

## What to do

Reduce water use, keep people away from surfacing wastewater, do not enter a tank, and call for assessment. If sewage is backing into the building, stop using plumbing fixtures until the system can be evaluated.

An inspection may find a full tank, blocked filter, failed pump, damaged line, distribution problem, hydraulic overload, or drain-field failure. The right repair depends on identifying which part failed.
