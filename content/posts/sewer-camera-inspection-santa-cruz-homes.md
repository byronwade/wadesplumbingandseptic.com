A sewer camera inspection is a recording of the inside of a drain or sewer line. A camera on a cable goes in through a cleanout and shows cracks, roots, grease, sags and separated joints, along with the distance from the access point. It does not repair the pipe. It tells you whether the next step is cleaning, a spot repair, a larger replacement or leaving the line alone. ## Quick answer

- Use a camera when the same clog keeps coming back, several fixtures are slow, you need to know if jetting is safe, or a sale or insurance question needs a record of the line.
- The recording is only as good as the access. A main-line cleanout makes the inspection and later cleaning much easier.
- In the Santa Cruz County Sanitation District, the property owner owns the sewer lateral, including the part under the street.
- Camera first, then clean or repair. Jetting a broken pipe can make a bad spot worse.

## What the camera can show

- **Grease and sludge** coating the pipe wall, the usual leftover after a snake has punched a hole through a clog
- **Root hair** at joints or cracks, common on older clay or concrete laterals near trees
- **Separated joints, cracks and broken sections** that leak sewage out and let soil in
- **Sags or bellies** that hold water and solids even after a cleaning
- **Collapsed or crushed pipe** that no amount of cleaning will open
- **The distance** from the cleanout to each defect, which is what makes a repair plan possible

A camera does not measure tank sludge on a septic system. For that you need a [septic inspection](/service-offerings/septic-tank-inspection-and-assessment). It can, though, show the pipe from the house to the tank when that run is the clog.

## When it is worth doing

- Recurring clogs in the same line after plunging or snaking
- Several fixtures slow or backing up at once
- Before [hydro jetting](/the-benefits-of-hydro-jetting), to confirm the pipe is intact
- After a backup, so you know whether the cause was grease, roots or a broken pipe
- Before buying or selling a home on public sewer, when the district or a lender wants a record of the lateral
- When wet soil, lush grass or odors show up along the buried line

## Access: the cleanout matters

The camera needs an opening. That is usually a cleanout in the yard, crawl space or garage. If there is no cleanout, the inspection may have to start through a fixture, which limits how far the camera can travel and how clearly it can see. A [main line cleanout installation](/service-offerings/main-line-cleanout-installation) is often the first recommendation, and in the Santa Cruz County Sanitation District a cleanout is part of the lateral rules that apply when a property is sold.

## Public sewer versus septic

**On public sewer** in the Santa Cruz County Sanitation District, County Public Works tells property owners that they own and are responsible for the sewer lateral from the building to the public main, including cleaning, leak repair and overflow cleanup. The district's lateral program FAQ is the place to read sale, inspection and transfer-of-responsibility details. Camera footage is the usual way to document the condition of that pipe.

**On septic**, the camera looks at the line to the tank, and sometimes the lines after the tank, depending on access. Slow drains throughout the house can also be the tank or drainfield. Pair the camera with a septic inspection instead of assuming the pipe is the whole story. See [septic trouble signs](/septic-trouble-signs-santa-cruz).

## What happens during the appointment

1. Locate the cleanout and protect floors or landscaping around it.
2. Run the camera and watch the recording in real time.
3. Note defects and their distance from the access point.
4. Decide the next step: leave it, [drain cleaning](/service-offerings/drain-cleaning) or hydro jetting if the pipe is sound, or [pipe repair](/service-offerings/pipe-repair-and-replacement) if it is not.

Ask for a copy of the recording. It is useful for a second opinion, a later repair and, on a sewer lateral, a sale file.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will a camera inspection clear my clog?

No. It diagnoses the line. Cleaning or repair is a separate step. Sometimes the camera is run after a cleaning so the walls are visible.

### Is hydro jetting safe after a camera inspection?

It can be, if the pipe is intact. If the camera shows a crack, collapse or badly separated joint, jetting is the wrong tool. The hydro jetting guide explains that split.

### Do I need a camera inspection to sell my house?

It depends on the sewer agency and the deal. In the Santa Cruz County Sanitation District, the owner is responsible for the lateral, and the district's sale process can require inspection or a transfer of responsibility. Ask the district or your escrow officer, and keep the recording.
