An engineered septic system is designed around the property's soil, slope, groundwater, setbacks, available area, wastewater flow, and treatment requirements.

## Why engineered treatment may be required

- A steep or constrained lot
- Poor or shallow soil
- High groundwater
- Environmental setbacks
- A failed existing system
- A project with higher design flow
- Local rules requiring improved treatment

## Components can include

Multiple tanks, pumps, floats, control panels, alarms, aerobic treatment, media filtration, disinfection, timed dosing, pressure distribution, drip dispersal, or mound components.

## The project process

1. Property and wastewater-flow review
2. Site and soil evaluation
3. Engineering and equipment selection
4. Health-department review and permitting
5. Excavation and installation
6. Electrical, controls, startup, and testing
7. Owner training and maintenance planning

Advanced systems can make difficult properties usable, but they must be installed and maintained as designed. Keep service records and respond to alarms promptly.
