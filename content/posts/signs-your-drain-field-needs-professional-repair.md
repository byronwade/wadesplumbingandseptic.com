The drain field—also called a leach field—is one of the most important parts of the septic system. When it stops accepting and treating wastewater, the entire property can be affected.

## Greener grass is not always good

Bright green strips, wet or mushy soil, odors, or dark material over the field can indicate wastewater reaching the surface.

## Multiple slow drains or gurgling fixtures

A single slow sink is often a local blockage. Slow drainage throughout the house, especially after the tank and building sewer are checked, can point to downstream restriction or a saturated field.

## Toilet and backup symptoms

Bubbling toilets, repeated backups at low fixtures, or sewage odor can signal that wastewater has nowhere to go.

## Common causes

- Excessive water loading
- Solids leaving an overdue or damaged tank
- A blocked filter or distribution component
- Crushed, rooted, or settled field piping
- Compacted or saturated soil
- An old, undersized, or poorly located field

Do not excavate or drive equipment over a suspected failed field. A professional assessment should separate a serviceable component problem from a field that needs repair, expansion, or replacement.
