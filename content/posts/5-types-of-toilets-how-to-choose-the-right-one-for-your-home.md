Every new toilet sold in California has to meet the same water limit, so the real choices are how it flushes, how it fits the room and who will use it. This guide covers five common types, the rules that apply in California, and what to measure before you buy.

## First, the water limit in California

Under California Energy Commission rules, toilets sold in California since January 1, 2016 may use no more than 1.28 gallons per flush. That is stricter than the federal standard of 1.6 gallons. EPA says older toilets can use as much as 6 gallons per flush, and that toilets account for nearly 30 percent of an average home's indoor water use.

California also has a rule for older toilets. Civil Code Section 1101.4 says single-family homes built on or before January 1, 1994 must have their noncompliant fixtures replaced, which includes any toilet made to use more than 1.6 gallons per flush. Starting in 2014, a building permit for alterations or improvements to such a home has required replacing noncompliant fixtures before final approval, and starting in 2017 sellers have had to disclose the requirement and whether the home has any noncompliant fixtures.

## 1. Single flush gravity toilets

The most common type. Water drops from the tank into the bowl and gravity does the work. In California a new one uses 1.28 gallons or less every flush. They have few parts, are quiet, and replacement parts like flappers and fill valves are easy to find. If yours runs between flushes, see our [running toilet guide](/santa-cruz-toilet-running-solutions).

## 2. Dual flush toilets

Dual flush toilets have two buttons or a split button: a smaller flush for liquid waste and a full flush for solids. For a WaterSense label, EPA requires that neither the full nor the reduced flush exceed 1.28 gallons. They save water when the reduced flush is used most of the time.

## 3. Push button toilets

Push button describes the handle, not the flushing system. A button on the tank lid, like the one on the toilet in the photo above, replaces the side lever, and many push button toilets are dual flush. The buttons are easy to clean. If the button stops working, the linkage or cable under the lid usually needs adjusting or replacing.

## 4. Pressure assisted toilets

A sealed vessel inside the tank uses the home's water pressure to push the flush harder. They clear the bowl with force and are common in commercial buildings, but they are louder than gravity toilets and the inner vessel has its own replacement parts.

## 5. Touchless toilets

A sensor starts the flush when you wave a hand or step away, so there is no handle to touch. Most use batteries or need an outlet near the toilet, so plan for that before installation. Many have a manual backup button for when the batteries die.

## How to compare toilets

- **Look for the WaterSense label.** To earn it, EPA requires a toilet to completely clear the test media in a single flush in at least four of five attempts, on top of the 1.28 gallon limit. EPA also excludes toilets with settings that let a user override the rated flush volume.
- **Seat height.** Standard toilets are lower. Chair or comfort height bowls are closer to the 17 to 19 inch seat height that the 2010 ADA Standards require for accessible toilets, which many people find easier to use.
- **Bowl shape.** Round bowls take less floor space. Elongated bowls are longer and many people find them more comfortable.
- **One piece or two piece.** A one piece toilet has the tank and bowl molded together, with fewer seams to clean. A two piece toilet is lighter to carry and often easier to repair.
- **Rough-in.** Measure from the finished wall behind the toilet to the center of the floor bolts and buy a toilet made for that distance. If the measurement does not match a standard size, ask before you buy.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many gallons per flush can a new toilet use in California?

No more than 1.28 gallons per flush for toilets sold in California since January 1, 2016.

### Do I have to replace an old toilet in California?

Under Civil Code Section 1101.4, single-family homes built on or before January 1, 1994 were required to replace toilets made to use more than 1.6 gallons per flush by January 1, 2017. Replacement is also a condition of final approval for permitted alterations, and sellers must disclose noncompliant fixtures.

### What is the difference between a push button and a dual flush toilet?

Push button describes how you flush. Dual flush means the toilet has two flush volumes. Many push button toilets are also dual flush.
