An enhanced treatment septic system in Santa Cruz County needs more than a construction permit. The owner must keep a contract with a county-approved Onsite System Service Provider (OSSP), record operating conditions on the deed, and pay an annual county oversight fee on the property tax bill. Wade's Plumbing & Septic installs these systems alongside the project's engineer. Wade's does not pump tanks and does not replace the OSSP for required sampling and service reports. Quotes are free.

## Quick answer

- Enhanced (alternative) systems are used when a conventional tank and gravity leach field cannot meet soil, groundwater, slope, or setback rules. That is common in [Felton](/service-area/felton-ca-plumbing-septic-services), [Ben Lomond](/service-area/ben-lomond-ca-plumbing-septic-services), and [Aptos](/service-area/aptos-ca-plumbing-septic-services).
- Before Environmental Health finals the sewage disposal permit, the owner signs a service contract with a qualified OSSP and an acknowledgment of special operating conditions. Telemetry is required.
- After construction, EH records a notice on the deed. An annual fee for county oversight appears on the property tax bill. Dollar amounts are set by the county, not by Wade's.
- The OSSP, not the installer, submits annual operation and maintenance reports and treated-effluent sample results. Large systems report quarterly.
- Tanks are pumped only by a county-registered liquid waste hauler. Wade's does not pump tanks.

## Why the extra paperwork exists

Santa Cruz County's [enhanced treatment regulations](https://scceh.com/Portals/6/Env_Health/LAMP/AppDEnhancedTreatment.pdf) (LAMP Appendix D) exist because many parcels cannot meet conventional OWTS standards. Shallow groundwater, soils that perc too fast or too slow, tight lots, and stream setbacks are the usual reasons. County Code sections 7.38.182 through 7.38.186 allow alternative technology and enhanced treatment on those sites.

The county's [OWTS maintenance page](https://scceh.com/NewHome/Programs/LandUse/MaintenanceofanOWTS.aspx) states that enhanced treatment systems need more intensive monitoring, and that the parcel owner **must** contract with an OSSP. EH keeps the list of approved providers. For proprietary units, the provider is certified by the manufacturer or dealer for the model on the property.

That OSSP contract is separate from the construction contract. Wade's [installs engineered systems](/service-offerings/engineered-septic-system-installation) to the permitted plan. Ongoing sampling and service reports go through the OSSP on the county list.

## What you sign before the permit is approved

Appendix D requires, before permit approval:

- A complete design by a qualified professional (California REHS, PE/CE, or PG), with perc data, groundwater, setbacks, pump calculations, and design flow.
- A signed contract with a qualified service provider for ongoing maintenance.
- An electrical permit from the Planning Department when the system has electrical components.
- Telemetry installed, with a contract that covers ongoing maintenance of that monitoring.
- An owner acknowledgment that describes how the system works, how it must be operated, use restrictions, and the annual county oversight fee on the tax bill.

Construction inspections typically include a preconstruction meeting (designer, provider, manufacturer, contractor, and EH), a watertight tank test, leach field installation, and a final check of the control panel, pressure test, alarm, telemetry, and erosion control. The OSSP files a startup report. The designer files a letter of completion (and an as-built if required). After the electrical permit is signed off, EH finals the sewage disposal permit and records the notice of special operating conditions on the deed.

## What you have to keep doing

The recorded notice sets the operation, maintenance, and monitoring rules for that system. Appendix D then requires:

- An operations and maintenance manual for the owner, the operator, and EH. The owner has to follow it.
- For proprietary systems, a two-year initial service policy with four site visits (every six months). After that period, an extended service policy is offered for a fee set by the provider, not by Wade's.
- Annual treated-effluent samples collected by the service provider, with results sent to EH with the service report. Residential reports are annual. Large systems (more than five dwelling units, or 2,500 gallons per day or more) report quarterly.
- Sample analysis by a certified laboratory. The panel includes BOD5 and TSS. Nitrogen tests apply when the system is permitted for nitrogen reduction. E. coli applies when pathogen reduction is required.
- A resample within 90 days if results miss the standard.
- A sample six months after installation, and again six months after a change of owner or use.
- Annual flushing of pressure laterals or drip tubing, and visual inspection of the dispersal area.
- Telemetry that can flag high and low liquid levels, excessive pump run time, and manufacturer-specified alarms.

If annual servicing, sampling, or passing results are missing, EH can assess a violation reinspection fee and record a Notice of Violation. Those amounts are county fees.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need an operating permit for a conventional septic tank?

The extra OSSP contract, deed notice, telemetry, and tax-bill oversight fee described here apply to enhanced treatment and alternative dispersal systems. Conventional systems still need a sewage disposal permit for new work, repairs, or upgrades, and they still need periodic pumping by a registered hauler. Wade's does not pump tanks.

### Can I skip the OSSP if I maintain the system myself?

Only if Environmental Health approves you as a qualified individual or certified homeowner on the OSSP program. Appendix D allows that path; it is not automatic. Most owners keep a manufacturer-certified provider on the county list.

### Does Wade's pump the tank or file the annual sample?

No. Wade's installs septic systems alongside the project's engineer and does not pump tanks. Annual samples and service reports are OSSP work. Pumping is done by a county-registered liquid waste hauler.
