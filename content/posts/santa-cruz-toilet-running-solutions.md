A toilet that keeps running wastes water, raises the utility bill, and can mask a bigger fill or flush problem. In Santa Cruz County homes—especially older bathrooms and vacation rentals—the usual culprits are a worn flapper, a misadjusted chain, a failing fill valve, or mineral buildup from hard water.

## Quick checks before you call

1. **Listen after a flush.** If water runs for more than about a minute, the tank is not sealing or the fill valve is not shutting off.
2. **Mark the tank water line.** Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and wait 10–15 minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper (or flush valve seat) is leaking.
3. **Watch the overflow tube.** Water spilling into the center overflow tube usually means the float or fill valve is set too high or sticking.
4. **Check the chain.** A chain that is too short holds the flapper open; a chain that is too long can catch under the flapper.

## Common Santa Cruz County causes

Hard water and mineral scale can keep flappers from seating cleanly. Older fluidmaster-style fill valves also fail more often in homes that sit unused between seasons. If the toilet rocks on the floor or the base is wet, stop DIY adjustments and call for a flange or wax-ring inspection—running water in the tank is a different problem from a floor leak.

## Repair vs. replace

Flapper and fill-valve replacements are often inexpensive and restore a quiet tank. Replace the toilet when the bowl is cracked, the tank is mismatched or sweating heavily, the trapway clogs repeatedly, or you want a modern high-efficiency model that still clears reliably.
