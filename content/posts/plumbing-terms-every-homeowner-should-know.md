Knowing a few plumbing words makes it easier to describe a problem on the phone, follow what a plumber recommends, and compare written bids. These are the terms homeowners hear most, grouped by the part of the house they belong to. For a longer A to Z reference, see the [plumbing glossary](/glossary/plumbing) and [septic glossary](/glossary/septic).

## Water supply terms

### Potable water

Water that is safe to drink. In a home it comes from a public water system or a private well, and the supply pipes that carry it must stay separate from drains and from anything that could contaminate them.

### Main shutoff valve

The valve that turns off water to the whole house. It is usually where the supply line enters the home or near the meter. Every adult in the house should know where it is and how to close it.

### Fixture shutoff (angle stop)

A small valve under a sink or behind a toilet that turns off water to that one fixture, so it can be repaired without shutting off the whole house.

### Pressure regulator (PRV)

A valve on the main line that lowers incoming water pressure from the street to a level that is safer for pipes, fixtures and appliances. Banging pipes or a dripping relief valve on the water heater can be signs it needs attention.

### PEX

Cross-linked polyethylene, a flexible plastic tubing used for water supply lines and for radiant floor heating, like the tubing in the photo above. It bends around corners with fewer fittings than rigid pipe.

### Hard water

USGS defines hardness as the amount of dissolved calcium and magnesium in water. Hard water leaves scale in water heaters and pipes and forms soap scum. USGS classes water above 180 milligrams per liter (as calcium carbonate) as very hard.

### Water softener

A device that treats hard water to reduce scale. Our comparison of [water softeners and whole house filtration](/water-softeners-vs-whole-house-filtration-systems-which-is-right-for-your-home) explains how the two differ.

## Drain and vent terms

### Trap and trap seal

The trap is the curved section of pipe under a drain. The trap seal is the water that stays in that curve and blocks sewer gas from coming up into the room. A drain that is rarely used can dry out and let odors through. Running water for a few seconds refills it.

### Vent

Pipes that let air into the drain system, usually through the roof. Vents keep water flowing smoothly and keep trap seals from being siphoned out. Gurgling drains can be a sign of a vent or drain problem.

### Branch drain

The pipe that carries wastewater from one fixture or a group of fixtures to the main drain.

### Cleanout

A capped access fitting on a drain or sewer line that lets a plumber run a cable or camera into the pipe without removing a toilet. Many homes have one outside near the foundation.

### Sewer lateral

The pipe from the house to the public sewer main. In Santa Cruz County the property owner is responsible for maintaining it. Our post on [dirty water backing up into a shower](/why-is-dirty-water-backing-up-into-your-shower) covers lateral blockages.

### Fittings and tees

Fittings join pipes and change their direction or size. Common ones are elbows, couplings and tees. A tee is a T-shaped fitting that joins three pipes.

## Septic terms

### Blackwater and greywater

Blackwater is wastewater from toilets. Greywater is used water from sources such as showers, tubs, bathroom sinks and clothes washers. In California, Chapter 15 of the California Plumbing Code sets the rules for reusing greywater for irrigation. A simple clothes washer (laundry to landscape) system can be built without a construction permit if it meets the code's conditions, such as no connection to the drinking water system, no pump other than the washer's own, no ponding or runoff, and a valve that can send water back to the sewer. Local agencies may add their own rules, so check before building one.

### Septic tank, sludge and scum

EPA describes a septic tank as a buried tank where solids settle to the bottom as sludge and oil and grease float to the top as scum.

### Effluent

The liquid that leaves the septic tank and flows to the drainfield.

### Drainfield (leach field)

EPA describes the drainfield as a shallow, covered excavation in unsaturated soil where the liquid from the tank filters through the soil. If it is overloaded with too much water it can flood, and sewage can surface in the yard or back up into the house. Keep vehicles off the drainfield and direct roof water away from it. See [how to care for a septic system](/how-to-maintain-your-septic-tank-for-long-term-performance) for more.

## Fixture and efficiency terms

### GPF (gallons per flush)

How much water a toilet uses each flush. EPA notes the federal standard is 1.6 gallons per flush, and toilets with the WaterSense label use 1.28 or less. California is stricter: under California Energy Commission rules, toilets sold in the state since January 1, 2016 may use no more than 1.28 gallons per flush. EPA says older toilets can use as much as 6 gallons per flush.

### GPM (gallons per minute)

The flow rate for faucets and showerheads. Our [bathroom faucet guide](/how-to-install-a-new-bathroom-faucet-a-weekend-diy-project) covers California's limits for bathroom faucets.

## Tool and material terms

### Snake (drain auger)

A flexible steel cable that is fed into a drain to break up or pull out a clog. Small hand snakes reach sink and tub traps. Longer machine cables are used for main lines and laterals.

### Blow bag (drain bladder)

A rubber bladder that attaches to a garden hose, expands to seal inside the pipe, and pushes pulses of water against a clog. It works best on short runs. On an old or damaged line, or a full main line blockage, a cable or camera inspection is the safer choice.

### Pipe dope and thread tape

Pipe dope (pipe joint compound) and thread tape seal threaded connections. Use only products rated for the job, since some are not approved for gas lines or drinking water. Thread tape is not used on compression fittings.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between blackwater and greywater?

Blackwater comes from toilets. Greywater is used water from sources such as showers, bathroom sinks and clothes washers. California's plumbing code allows some greywater to be reused for irrigation under set conditions.

### How much water does a toilet use per flush in California?

Toilets sold in California since January 1, 2016 may use no more than 1.28 gallons per flush. Older toilets can use far more.

### What does a cleanout do?

It gives a plumber direct access to a drain or sewer line so a cable or camera can be run into the pipe without pulling a toilet.
