Good septic maintenance protects the tank, treatment process, mechanical equipment, and dispersal field.

## Know the system

Keep permits, drawings, inspection reports, pumping records, equipment manuals, and the location of tanks and field components. Never enter a septic tank.

## Inspect and pump

Schedule inspections based on system type and local requirements. Pump when solids measurements and household loading indicate it—not only after a backup.

## Maintain filters and equipment

Effluent filters, pumps, floats, alarms, control panels, aerobic units, and disinfection components need the service specified for the system.

## Use water evenly

Repair leaks and spread high-volume activities across the week. Hydraulic overload can force wastewater through the tank too quickly or saturate the field.

## Keep harmful material out

Flush only human waste and appropriate toilet paper. Keep wipes, grease, chemicals, medicines, paint, and non-degradable products out of the system.

## Protect the field

Divert runoff, avoid vehicles and structures, and use shallow-rooted landscaping.

Call when you notice alarms, odors, backups, gurgling, or surfacing wastewater. Early diagnosis gives the owner more options.
