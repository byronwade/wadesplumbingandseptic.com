## Quick answer

- Not every house has a backflow preventer. You have one if your water system required it for a cross-connection hazard.
- California's Cross-Connection Control Policy Handbook requires field testing after installation, repair, winterizing (depressurization) or permanent relocation, and at least annually after that.
- The tester must hold certification from an organization the State Water Board recognizes.
- If the assembly fails, the Handbook requires repair or replacement within 30 days of notice, unless your water system allows a documented extension.
- Your water district, not the plumber, sets how results are reported.

## What backflow is

Backflow is water reversing direction in a pipe. It can pull irrigation water, stagnant hose water or other non-potable water toward the drinking-water supply when pressure drops or a downstream pump overpowers the street main. A backflow prevention assembly is a mechanical device, often near the meter, the irrigation point of connection, or a well interconnection, that is designed to stop that reversal.

You cannot tell whether the internal checks still seal by looking at the outside of the device. That is why the test is a field procedure, not a visual glance.

## What California requires

The State Water Resources Control Board's Cross-Connection Control Policy Handbook took effect July 1, 2024, and was amended April 21, 2026. It requires public water systems to protect their distribution system with a cross-connection control program. For backflow prevention assemblies installed to protect the public water system, the Handbook says the water system must ensure that:

- Assemblies are field tested after installation, repair, depressurization for winterizing, or permanent relocation
- Testing is done by certified backflow prevention assembly testers
- Assemblies are field tested at least annually, and more often if the water system, the State Water Board or a local health agency requires it for a higher hazard
- Failed assemblies are repaired or replaced within 30 days of notification of the failure, unless the water system's Cross-Connection Control Plan allows an extension

The Handbook also says testers must notify the water system as soon as possible within 24 hours if they observe a backflow incident or an unprotected cross-connection during the test.

## How local districts apply that

Each water system writes its own ordinance or policy on top of the Handbook. One local example is Soquel Creek Water District Ordinance 25-01 (June 3, 2025), which covers much of [Capitola](/service-area/capitola-ca-plumbing-septic-services), [Aptos](/service-area/aptos-ca-plumbing-septic-services) and Soquel. That ordinance:

- Requires an approved assembly before service in listed hazard cases, including an auxiliary water supply (such as a well), a booster pump, or plumbing that handles sewage or recycled water
- Puts installation, upkeep and annual testing on the customer, at the customer's expense
- Requires the annual test to be done by a person the District approves, with records reported to the District Cross-Connection Control Specialist
- Allows the District to test the assembly and bill the customer if the customer does not have the test done

Other Santa Cruz County water systems have their own notice letters and report forms. Follow the letter from your provider even if a neighbor's district does something slightly different.

Santa Cruz County Environmental Health keeps a [list of certified backflow prevention device testers](https://scceh.com/Portals/6/Env_Health/water_resources/DrinkingH2O/Backflow%20Prevention%20Device%20Testers.pdf) as a reference. The county says it does not recommend any company on the list over any other, listed or unlisted, and that the water system using the list is responsible for checking qualifications.

## Winterizing and irrigation

Coastal yards still winterize irrigation. The Handbook treats depressurization for winterizing as a reason to field test the assembly afterward, not only on the calendar anniversary. If a device is taken apart, repaired or moved, it needs a test before it is left in service.

Insulating an above-ground backflow preventer on a cold night is a separate freeze-protection step. See [how to prevent frozen pipes](/how-to-prevent-frozen-pipes-this-winter).

## What to have ready for the tester

- The make, model and serial number on the assembly, or a clear photo of the tag
- The water district account number and any test form the district mailed
- Clear access to the device, including vegetation, padlocks and dog gates
- A hose bib nearby if the tester's procedure needs a water source

If the assembly fails, ask for the failed test report before anyone starts repairs, then get a passing test into the district's records within the deadline your provider gave you.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do all Santa Cruz County homes need backflow testing?

No. Testing applies to assemblies the water system required for a cross-connection hazard. Many houses have none. If you received an annual test notice, you have one.

### How often is the test due?

At least annually under the Cross-Connection Control Policy Handbook, and also after installation, repair, winterizing or relocation. Your water system can require more frequent testing.

### Who is allowed to perform the test?

A person with valid certification from an organization the State Water Board recognizes. Some districts also keep their own approved-tester list.

### What happens if the device fails?

The Handbook requires repair or replacement within 30 days of notice unless your water system's plan allows a longer period. Then it has to pass a new field test.
