A new or replacement septic system in unincorporated Santa Cruz County is designed for the parcel, permitted by County Environmental Health, and installed to that approved design. County Code Chapter 7.38 and the Local Agency Management Program already set those rules. Many rural lots, including steep, sandy, or high-groundwater sites, need an enhanced treatment system rather than a conventional tank and leach field.

Santa Cruz LAFCO has reported that on the order of 22,000 to 24,000 rural homes in the county are served by septic systems ([Santa Cruz LAFCO](https://santacruzlafco.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/4a4-CSA-12-Review.pdf)). This guide covers why enhanced treatment is common here, how permitting and sale inspections work, and two treatment units you will see on local designs: MicroSepTec EnviroServer and Orenco AdvanTex. For the step by step install path, start with [new septic installations in Santa Cruz County](/new-septic-installations-santa-cruz). Wade's Plumbing & Septic installs systems alongside the project's engineer. Wade's does not pump tanks.

## Why new systems are common here

Aging tanks, hillside soils, and tighter water-quality rules all push Santa Cruz County toward better treatment:

- **Water quality.** Failing or undersized systems can send bacteria and nitrogen toward groundwater, creeks, and coastal aquifers. County enhanced-treatment rules exist to keep that effluent cleaner before it reaches soil.
- **Site conditions.** Small lots, steep slopes, high groundwater, and sandy soils are common in the San Lorenzo Valley and along the coast. A conventional gravity field often cannot meet setbacks or soil-depth rules on those parcels. Our post on [how Santa Cruz County terrain affects septic systems](/how-santa-cruz-countys-unique-geography-affects-septic-systems) covers the slope, groundwater, and soil rules in more detail.
- **Rebuilds and additions.** New construction, fire rebuilds, bedroom additions, and accessory dwelling units have to meet current standards, not the rules the original tank was built under.

County Environmental Health, not the installer, decides whether a conventional system is allowed. If the site cannot support one, the design uses enhanced treatment and a dispersal method the county will approve.

## Permits, design, and who does what

Any new septic system or significant repair needs a sewage disposal permit from County Environmental Health. A qualified professional evaluates the site (soil profiles, percolation tests, setbacks) and prepares the design. The county's [qualified-professional list](https://scceh.com/NewHome/Programs/LandUse/QualifiedProfessionals.aspx) is the official split:

- **Site evaluation and design:** California registered civil engineer, professional geologist, registered environmental health specialist, or another professional the county approves.
- **Installation:** a contractor licensed in Class A, Class B, C-36, or C-42. A Class B holder may install a system only as part of new construction.
- **Enhanced-system maintenance:** an onsite system service provider approved by the county and certified by the manufacturer.

Wade's holds California contractor license CSLB #1087260 (C-36 Plumbing, C-42 Sanitation System) and installs to the approved design. Wade's does not replace the engineer and does not pump tanks.

The county inspects the installation. A copy of the approved design stays on site until Environmental Health signs off. See [OWTS New Development](https://scceh.com/NewHome/Programs/LandUse/OWTSNewDevelopment.aspx) and [OWTS Repair and Upgrades](https://scceh.com/NewHome/Programs/LandUse/OWTSRepairUpgrades.aspx).

## Point-of-sale inspection

Since July 1, 2023, sellers of Santa Cruz County homes with septic systems must have the system inspected and the tank emptied by a registered liquid waste hauler, then give the buyer a standard disclosure. Needed repairs are the seller's responsibility unless buyer and seller agree otherwise in writing. Wade's inspects, helps with permits, and installs repairs or replacements with the project's engineer. Wade's does not empty tanks. ## Treatment units you will see on local designs

The engineer specifies the treatment unit. Two products that show up often on Santa Cruz County designs are the MicroSepTec EnviroServer and the Orenco AdvanTex. County rules do not pick a brand. They require treatment performance, a maintenance contract, and a design that fits the parcel.

- **MicroSepTec EnviroServer.** A packaged treatment tank that treats wastewater in stages before dispersal. Manufacturer information is on the [MicroSepTec site](https://microseptec.com/).
- **Orenco AdvanTex.** A textile-filter treatment pod that sits after the septic tank and treats effluent before it goes to the field. Manufacturer information is on the [Orenco treatment-systems page](https://www.orenco.com/products/treatment-systems).

Both still need a septic tank (or primary chamber), a dispersal field or drip system, power for pumps or blowers, and a service contract. For how those pieces fit together, see [what you need to know about engineered septic systems](/what-you-need-to-know-about-engineered-septic-systems) and [septic system types in Santa Cruz County](/septic-system-types-santa-cruz).

## Caring for an enhanced system

County enhanced-treatment rules require an annual service agreement with a qualified provider. That contract covers inspections, filter service, and mechanical checks. It does not replace tank emptying.

- **Empty the tank when the inspection says to.** EPA's homeowner guidance is a typical interval of three to five years, based on household size, wastewater volume, solids, and tank size. Wade's does not pump tanks. Book a licensed liquid waste hauler. Environmental Health publishes a [list of permitted pumper companies](https://www.scceh.com/Portals/6/Env_Health/Land%20Use/Pumper%20Documents/PumperTruckCompanies.pdf).
- **Keep trash and chemicals out of the system.** Only toilet paper and wastewater belong in the tank. Wipes, grease, solvents, and leftover medicines disrupt treatment.
- **Stagger heavy water use.** Spread laundry and showers so the system is not hit with one large dose.
- **Protect the field.** Do not drive, pave, or plant deep-rooted trees over the tank or drainfield.
- **Keep records.** Permits, design drawings, inspection reports, pumping receipts, and service logs are required at sale.

More homeowner steps are in the [complete guide to septic system maintenance](/the-complete-guide-to-septic-system-maintenance).

## Frequently asked questions

### Who designs a new septic system in Santa Cruz County?

County Environmental Health says soil studies, percolation tests, and system designs are prepared by a California registered civil engineer, professional geologist, registered environmental health specialist, or another professional the county approves. Wade's installs to that design and does not replace the engineer.

### Do I need a permit to install or replace a septic system?

Yes. The county requires a sewage disposal permit to install, upgrade, enlarge, replace, repair, or destroy an onsite wastewater treatment system. See [OWTS New Development](https://scceh.com/NewHome/Programs/LandUse/OWTSNewDevelopment.aspx).

### Does Wade's pump septic tanks?

No. Wade's installs septic systems alongside engineers and inspects or repairs them. Tank pumping is done by a licensed liquid waste hauler.
