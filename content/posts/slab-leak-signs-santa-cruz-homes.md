A slab leak is a break in a water supply line that runs in or under the concrete floor. You usually cannot see the pipe. You see the effects: a warm or damp patch of floor, a water meter that moves when every fixture is off, or a bill that climbs with no change in how you use water. Finding it early matters because wet materials that stay wet grow mold. ## Quick answer

- Confirm a hidden leak with the water meter test: turn everything off, read the meter, wait two hours with no water use, and read it again. EPA WaterSense says that if the reading changed, you probably have a leak.
- Signs that the leak is under the slab, not in a wall or a toilet, include a warm spot on the floor, damp concrete or carpet, and water that has no obvious source above it.
- EPA says the key to mold control is moisture control, and that wet or damp materials dried within 24 to 48 hours usually will not grow mold.
- In unincorporated Santa Cruz County, stopping a leak without replacing or rearranging pipe can be exempt from a plumbing permit. Replacing the under-slab line is replacement work and needs a permit.
- California Plumbing Code section 609.3 sets how water piping in or under a slab must be installed, including that galvanizing is not enough protection for ferrous pipe.

## What a slab leak is

Many Santa Cruz County houses put the water supply in a crawl space or in walls. Others, especially slab-on-grade houses in [Watsonville](/service-area/watsonville-ca-plumbing-septic-services), [Aptos](/service-area/aptos-ca-plumbing-septic-services) and parts of [Santa Cruz](/service-area/santa-cruz-ca-plumbing-septic-services), run hot and cold lines in or under the concrete. When one of those lines fails, water wets the slab, the soil under it, or both. The leak can be on a hot line, a cold line, or a joint.

It is still a supply leak, not a sewer leak. Dirty water backing up from a drain is a different problem. Our [hidden leak guide](/6-signs-you-have-a-hidden-water-leak) covers toilets, meters and well pumps. This post is for the case where the floor itself is the clue.

## Signs the leak is under the floor

Use more than one sign. A single warm tile can be a sun patch. Several of these together are a reason to test the meter and call for leak detection.

- **A floor that is warm in one spot** when the weather does not explain it, especially if the water heater has been running. That often means a hot-water line under the slab.
- **Damp concrete, damp carpet, or a musty smell at floor level** with no roof leak and no overflowed fixture above.
- **Soft or stained baseboards and lower cabinets** along an interior wall that has no plumbing in the wall above.
- **A water bill that jumps** without a change in household size or irrigation. EPA's leak checklist says a family of four using more than 12,000 gallons (or 16 CCF) in a winter month likely has a serious leak, and that a month-to-month spike is another reason to look.
- **The meter moves with everything off.** That is the confirmation test, described below.
- **A well pump that cycles when no one is using water.** Mountain and rural homes on a private well have no utility meter. The pump is the equivalent clue.

EPA WaterSense also tells homeowners to check walls, ceilings and floors for moisture or mold, because a pipe can leak out of sight and still waste water. The same checklist puts pooling at the water heater and under appliances on the list. Check those first so you do not open a slab for a leak that is actually at a flex line.

## Confirm it with the meter, then narrow the location

EPA's method is simple. Find the meter (usually near the curb, sometimes inside). Take a reading while no water is being used. Check it again after two hours with every fixture, appliance and irrigation off. If the numbers changed, water is going somewhere.

Then isolate what you can:

1. Shut the valves under sinks, behind toilets, and at the washing machine and refrigerator. If the meter stops, the leak is on a fixture supply, not under the slab.
2. If you have a whole-house shutoff after the meter, use it. If the meter still moves with that valve closed, the leak is between the meter and the house, which is a yard or service-line problem, not a slab problem. See [water line repair](/service-offerings/water-line-repair-and-installation).
3. If the meter stops only when you shut the house off at the slab or the first interior valve, the leak is inside the building footprint.

A plumber can then locate the break so the repair is a small opening or a reroute, not a guess. Do not keep using the line once you know it is leaking. Shut the affected hot or cold side if you can, and dry the wet area.

## Why drying the floor cannot wait

EPA's mold guidance is blunt: the key to mold control is moisture control, and it is important to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. If wet or damp materials are dried in that window, in most cases mold will not grow. A slab leak that soaks carpet pad, cabinets or the bottom of a wall can stay wet much longer than that if you only mop the surface.

Fix the pipe and dry the materials. Cleaning visible mold without stopping the leak is not a solution. EPA says if you clean up the mold but do not fix the water problem, the mold problem will most likely come back.

## Repair, reroute or re-pipe, and the permit

Once the leak is found, the choices are a spot repair in the slab, rerouting that run through a wall, cabinet or attic so it is no longer under the concrete, or replacing more of the supply piping. Which option fits depends on the pipe material, how many joints sit in the slab, and whether other lines are in the same condition. Old galvanized under a slab is a reason to look at more than one fitting. Our [re-pipe guide](/how-to-prepare-for-a-re-pipe-santa-cruz) covers whole-house replacement when the problem is throughout the house.

Santa Cruz County Building Permits says a plumbing permit is required when plumbing, gas or drainage piping, a fixture, or water heating or treatment equipment is installed, altered, repaired or replaced, unless the California Plumbing Code or County ordinance exempts the work. Exempt work includes stopping leaks in drains, soil, waste or vent pipe, clearing stoppages, and repairing leaks in pipes, valves or fixtures when the repair does not replace or rearrange those parts.

Replacing an under-slab water line, or rerouting it so the run is no longer under the floor, replaces or rearranges piping. That work needs a permit in the unincorporated county. If you live in [Santa Cruz](/service-area/santa-cruz-ca-plumbing-septic-services), [Capitola](/service-area/capitola-ca-plumbing-septic-services), [Scotts Valley](/service-area/scotts-valley-ca-plumbing-septic-services) or [Watsonville](/service-area/watsonville-ca-plumbing-septic-services), confirm the rule with that city's building department. The installer should pull the permit for the work they do.

## What the plumbing code requires under a slab

The 2025 California Plumbing Code (Title 24, Part 5), section 609.3, covers water piping installed within a building and in or under a concrete floor slab on the ground. "Within a building" means within the fixed limits of the building foundation. The section requires that:

- Ferrous piping have a machine-applied protective coating of an approved type. Field wrapping is only for short sections and fittings that had to be stripped for threading. Zinc coating (galvanizing) is not adequate protection for piping or fittings. Approved nonferrous piping does not have to be wrapped.
- Copper or copper alloy tubing be installed without joints where possible. Where joints are permitted, they must be brazed, and the fittings must be wrought copper.

That is the standard a new or replacement under-slab run has to meet. It is also why a leaking galvanized line in a slab is not a candidate for a wrapped patch and a hope. The replacement has to be a material and joint method the code allows in that location. Cities can adopt local amendments, so the inspector's adopted edition is the last word.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I know the leak is under the slab and not a toilet?

Do the meter test, then shut fixture valves. A toilet that leaks into its own bowl usually fails EPA's food-coloring test and will not warm a patch of floor in another room. A slab leak keeps moving the meter after toilets and faucets are isolated.

### Can a slab leak cause mold?

Yes, if the floor, pad or walls stay wet. EPA says wet materials should be dried within 24 to 48 hours, and that moisture control is how you control mold.

### Do I need a permit to fix a slab leak in Santa Cruz County?

If the work only stops a leak without replacing or rearranging the pipe, County Building Permits lists that as exempt. Replacing or rerouting the under-slab line is not that exemption. Confirm the rule if you are inside a city.
