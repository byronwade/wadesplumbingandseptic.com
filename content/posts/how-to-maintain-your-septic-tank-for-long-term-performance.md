A septic tank lasts longest when solids stay in the tank, liquid reaches the drainfield at a steady rate, and the parts that wear out get checked before they fail. For the full routine maintenance schedule, see our [septic system maintenance guide](/the-complete-guide-to-septic-system-maintenance). This post focuses on what shortens a tank's life and what helps it last.

## What wears out first

- **Baffles or tees.** These fittings at the inlet and outlet keep scum and solids in the tank. When one breaks or falls off, solids can flow out to the drainfield and clog it.
- **The effluent filter.** Many tanks have a screen at the outlet that catches solids before they leave the tank. Santa Cruz County's sewage disposal ordinance allows an approved effluent filter as the way to keep solids larger than 3/16 inch out of the dispersal system. A filter that is never cleaned clogs and can back water up into the house.
- **Lids and risers.** The county ordinance requires a riser from each manhole cover to the ground surface so the tank can be inspected and maintained. Cracked or loose lids let in rainwater and are a safety hazard.
- **Pumps, floats and alarms.** Systems with these parts, including treatment units like the one in the photo above, need more frequent service. EPA says systems with mechanical parts should generally be inspected once a year.
- **The tank itself.** Cracks or a failing tank let groundwater in and sewage out. Our guide to [septic system components](/septic-system-components-santa-cruz) explains each part.

## Habits that help a tank last

- **Get it inspected on schedule.** EPA says a typical household system should be inspected by a septic professional at least every three years. The inspection checks for leaks and measures the scum and sludge layers.
- **Empty the tank when the inspection says to.** EPA says household tanks are typically pumped every three to five years, depending on household size, wastewater volume, solids and tank size. Wade's Plumbing & Septic does not pump tanks, so book a licensed pumping company and have the filter and baffles checked while the tank is open.
- **Spread out water use.** Fix leaks and running toilets, and spread laundry across the week so the drainfield is not flooded in one day.
- **Keep solids and chemicals out.** Only human waste and toilet paper go in the toilet. Keep grease, wipes, cat litter, medicines and harsh chemicals out of drains.
- **Protect the drainfield.** EPA says not to park or drive on it, not to build over it or cover it with concrete or asphalt, and to plant grass rather than trees or shrubs near it.

## Myths that shorten a tank's life

### "Additives keep the tank healthy"

A tank already contains the bacteria it needs. Oregon State University Extension, drawing on EPA SepticSmart materials, says septic additives are unnecessary and can harm the system and the environment, and that many products lack independent research behind their claims. Biological additives can disrupt the treatment process, and chemical ones can contaminate groundwater. Save the money for an inspection.

### "If it drains, it is fine"

A system can drain normally while the baffles are gone and solids are moving into the drainfield. By the time drains slow or the yard is soggy, the drainfield may already be damaged. Our post on [septic trouble signs](/septic-trouble-signs-santa-cruz) lists what to watch for.

### "The softener can drain to the tank"

Not in Santa Cruz County. The county ordinance says discharge from water softeners and drinking water filters shall not be discharged to the septic system. Our [water softener versus filter guide](/water-softeners-vs-whole-house-filtration-systems-which-is-right-for-your-home) covers the alternatives.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do septic tanks need additives?

No. A tank already has the bacteria it needs. University extension guidance based on EPA materials says additives are unnecessary and can cause harm.

### How often should a septic tank be inspected?

EPA recommends at least every three years for a typical household system and generally once a year for systems with pumps, floats or other mechanical parts.

### What does an effluent filter do?

It catches solids at the tank outlet so they do not reach the drainfield. Santa Cruz County's ordinance allows an approved effluent filter to keep solids larger than 3/16 inch out of the dispersal system.
