## Scheduled septic pumping protects the system

Solids and scum accumulate even in a healthy septic tank. If too much material builds up, it can reduce treatment volume, block components, or move toward the drain field.

The appropriate service interval depends on tank capacity, number of occupants, wastewater use, garbage-disposal use, system design, prior solids measurements, and maintenance history.

## What the visit can include

- Locating and safely exposing required tank access
- Pumping the intended tank compartments
- Observing accessible baffles, tees, lids, and tank conditions
- Cleaning an accessible effluent filter when included
- Documenting unusual material, damage, or maintenance concerns
- Recommending risers, repairs, inspection, or a future service interval

Pumping does not by itself certify the drain field or prove every buried component is working correctly. Slow drains, alarms, odors, surfacing wastewater, or repeated backups may require additional diagnostics.

Keep pumping and inspection records with the property. Call before an emergency develops if the prior service date is unknown.
